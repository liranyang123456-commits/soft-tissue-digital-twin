Dear Editor,

We submit our manuscript "Physics-Audited Soft-Tissue Digital Twins from
Endoscopic Video: Canonical Gaussian Fields, Simulation-in-the-Loop
Elasticity Inversion, and Parameter Provenance" for consideration in
Computer Methods and Programs in Biomedicine.

Surgical data science and patient-specific planning need a simulable model
of soft tissue, not merely a visual reconstruction. Yet mechanical
parameters (Young's modulus, Poisson ratio) are not directly observable
from images, and existing endoscopic reconstruction methods either assign
them silently or report them as if measured. Our work addresses this gap
with two linked contributions.

First, we present a three-stage pipeline that converts monocular endoscopic
video into a physically simulable soft-tissue digital twin: a canonical
scene-reconstruction stage fits a Gaussian BRDF field with tool-aware
masking (36.8/34.1 dB tissue-masked PSNR on two EndoNeRF scenes, 26.6 dB on
real SCARED stereo data); a deformation-tracking stage follows the tissue
motion with a constant-velocity model and divergence rollback; and a
mechanical-inversion stage recovers elastic properties by differentiable
simulation-in-the-loop inversion.

Second, and central to the biomedical computing community, we make
parameter provenance a first-class, auditable output: every physical
parameter is tagged as measured, estimated, or assumed, with confidence. We
validate the mechanical-inversion stage on four stages, from a synthetic
closed loop (uniform modulus recovered to 0.01% error) through an
independent FEM-generated benchmark and a force-provenance study, to a real
ultrasound phantom with independent compression-test ground truth (modulus
contrast 3.80x vs. 3.56x, 6.9% error). A Laplace-posterior uncertainty
study correctly flags an unidentifiable inclusion, demonstrating that the
audit reports calibrated rather than overconfident estimates.

We believe this honest, validated route from endoscopic video to a
simulable soft-tissue twin fits the journal's focus on computational
methods and software systems for biomedicine. All results are reproducible
from the released code and public datasets
(https://github.com/liranyang123456-commits/soft-tissue-digital-twin).

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not
under consideration by any other journal. All authors have approved the
submission and declare no competing interests.

Thank you for your consideration.
